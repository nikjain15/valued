Why it exists

Most AI work starts at the model. Most AI failures start before it, or after the demo.

Before the model

The wrong thing gets built

  • Use cases are picked because a model can do them, not because the work needs it.
  • Nobody prices the problem, so nobody can say later whether it paid back.
  • Many ideas are rules or self-service, and never needed AI.
After the demo

Nobody can say if it works

  • There is no agreed answer key, so quality is an opinion.
  • Averages hide the language or customer group that breaks.
  • Nothing decided in advance what stops a release, and nothing watches it once it ships.

The method

Six gates. A use case clears each one before it moves on.

Questions

What people ask.

Is VALUED a product?

No. It is a method and the harness that runs it. The products it points to are prototypes and a platform, on synthetic data, with no users.

Does the workflow make the decisions?

No. It drafts tables and recommends. Every judgement call goes to the client as a card, and the brief records who chose what.

Which model does the prototype use?

None. The prototype it builds is deterministic on purpose: it proves the gates and the evals, and a model would sit behind them. Conduit shows the routing layer a model would come through.

Is any of the data real?

No. Cases, customers, members and clients are synthetic. Figures marked assumed are placeholders.

What if my case is not a list of AI ideas?

Start at the gate that fits. For a live AI feature that is underperforming, start at Deploy and drive and work backwards.

Why "VALUED"?

Value, AI-fit, Labels, User-in-the-loop, Evals, Deploy and drive. The order is the point: value and fit come before any model, and the work does not end at launch.
