Why it exists

Most AI work starts at the model. Most AI failures start before it, or after the demo.

Before the model

A use case is picked because a model can do it, not because the work needs it. Nobody prices the problem, so nobody can say later whether it paid back. Many of these ideas are rules or self-service, not AI.

After the demo

There is no agreed answer key, so quality is an opinion. Averages hide the language or customer group that breaks. Nobody decides in advance what stops a release, and nothing watches it once it ships.

The method

Six gates. A use case clears each one before it moves on.

Each gate answers one question, produces one artifact, and ends with one decision the client makes.

V

Value

Whose work or decision changes, how often, and what does it cost today?

Produces
A value frame and one north-star metric
Client decides
Which outcome this has to move first
A

AI-fit

Is it AI-shaped at all, what goes first, and at what cost, latency and quality?

Produces
Every idea on the least-AI ladder (rules, classic ML, retrieval and a model, an agent), ranked
Client decides
The first wave; build, buy or partner
L

Labels

What does right look like, written down before anything is built?

Produces
A golden set by slice, with a holdout the builder never sees
Client decides
Which slice worries them most
U

User-in-the-loop

Where does a person sit for every action, and what does the AI never do?

Produces
Autonomy per action, a never list, escalation triggers
Client decides
How much autonomy in the first 90 days
E

Evals

What number stops the release, set before the build?

Produces
Release gates by slice, a prototype, and a run that passes or blocks
Client decides
The bar for the riskiest miss; ship or fix
D

Deploy and drive

How is it launched, and how is it kept honest after launch?

Produces
Shadow, pilot and sign-off steps; drift, adoption and edit-rate metrics
Client decides
Pilot scope and what would make them stop

The loop: what production gets wrong becomes new labels. D feeds back into L.

How it runs

One line in. A decision record and a tested prototype out.

Step 1

Describe the case

Type one line: who, what problem, what constraint. Every number the workflow assumes is marked as assumed.

Step 2

Each gate drafts

A table first, never a blank question. At AI-fit, three scorers rate value, feasibility and risk in parallel, so no single lens wins by accident.

Step 3

The client decides

At most two questions per gate, two to four options each, the recommendation first with its reason.

Step 4

Brief and prototype

A builder writes a small prototype before it may see the answer key; the evals release it or block it. A one-page brief lists every decision.

Honest by design

The rules that keep the numbers true.

Labels before the modelThe answer key is written by intent, not by what the system outputs. Otherwise you grade the system against itself.
The builder never sees the holdoutA fix is scored twice: on the rows it learned from, and on rows it never saw.
Metrics by sliceAn average hides the broken language or customer group. Every gate is checked per slice.
Built or designed, always labelledAnything not running is marked designed, here and in the products.
Assumptions markedEvery figure the workflow assumes says so, to be replaced with real data in week one.
Escalation wins every raceIf a stop condition fires, nothing else is drafted.

Use it

Run it on your own case.

  1. Clone the repository. Seven skills (an orchestrator and one per gate) and two agents live in .claude/.
  2. Open it in Claude Code. The skills and agents load from the repository.
  3. Type one line. /valued followed by your case. Add --auto to accept every recommendation without questions.
git clone https://github.com/nikjain15/valued
cd valued && claude

/valued <one line describing your case>

Questions

What people ask.

Is VALUED a product?

No. It is a method and the harness that runs it. The products it points to are prototypes and a platform, on synthetic data, with no users.

Does the workflow make the decisions?

No. It drafts tables and recommends. Every judgement call goes to the client as a card, and the brief records who chose what.

Which model does the prototype use?

None. The prototype it builds is deterministic on purpose: it proves the gates and the evals, and a model would sit behind them. Conduit shows the routing layer a model would come through.

Is any of the data real?

No. Cases, customers, members and clients are synthetic. Figures marked assumed are placeholders.

What if my case is not a list of AI ideas?

Start at the gate that fits. For a live AI feature that is underperforming, start at Deploy and drive and work backwards.

Why "VALUED"?

Value, AI-fit, Labels, User-in-the-loop, Evals, Deploy and drive. The order is the point: value and fit come before any model, and the work does not end at launch.
